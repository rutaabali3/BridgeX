Huajiang Canyon Bridge is now the world’s highest deck

Changelog · Applied 27 November 2026 (record set 28 September 2025) · Pages updated: Highest Bridges

For nearly a decade the Duge Bridge (Beipanjiang) in Guizhou, China held the record for the highest bridge deck — about 565 m (1,854 ft) above the Beipan River, opened in 2016.

On 28 September 2025 the Huajiang Canyon Bridge (also called Huajiang Grand Canyon Bridge) opened on the S57 Liuzhi–Anlong Expressway, also in Guizhou. Its roadway sits about 625 m (2,051 ft) above the Beipan River — roughly 60 m higher than Duge, a short distance upstream on the same river system.

  • What changed on Highest Bridges: Promoted Huajiang Canyon Bridge to #1 (625 m, 2025) and moved Duge Bridge to #2 (565 m, 2016).
  • Specs: Suspension bridge · Total length ~2,890 m (9,480 ft) · Main span ~1,420 m (4,660 ft).
  • Note: Highest means deck height above the valley or water; tower height is tracked on Tallest Bridges. Real 16:9 imagery added on 27 November 2026.

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica — Huajiang Canyon Bridge

Highest Bridges Top 10 overhaul & card corrections

Changelog · Applied 27 November 2026 · Pages updated: Highest Bridges, Gallery, History

Updated the Top 10 ranking, metadata, and descriptions across our Highest Bridges listings and matching Gallery/History entries:

  • Tianmen Bridge (Guizhou, China) — Added at #3: Completed in 2026 on the Anpan Expressway at ~560 m (1,837 ft) deck height (820 m main span), ahead of Sidu River Bridge (496 m).
  • Zangkejiang Bridge (Guizhou, China) — Added at #8: Opened 14 February 2025 at ~410 m (1,345 ft) deck height (1,080 m main span), ahead of Qingshui River Bridge (406 m). Together with Huajiang and Tianmen, this moved Liuguanghe Xiqian (375 m) and Baling River Bridge (370 m) outside the Top 10.
  • Hegigio Gorge Pipeline Bridge — Removed from active Top 10 (#7): Heavily damaged in the 2018 Papua New Guinea earthquake and subsequently decommissioned/removed.
  • Baluarte Bridge (#10): Updated deck height 390 m (1,280 ft) → 403 m (1,322 ft) on Highest Bridges.
  • Jin’an Bridge (#6): Corrected route description Lijiang–Shangri-La Expressway → G4216 Chengdu–Lijiang (Huaping–Lijiang) Expressway on Highest Bridges.
  • Yachi River Bridge (#7): Clarified height baseline across Highest Bridges, Gallery, and History — 434 m (1,424 ft) above original canyon floor vs. 306 m (1,004 ft) above Dongfeng Dam reservoir, and harmonized length to 1,444 m.
  • Liuguanghe Bridge: Fixed conflation on History — separated the 2001 prestressed concrete beam bridge (297 m high, ~580 m long, 240 m span) from the 2017 Liuguanghe Xiqian Expressway cable-stayed bridge (375 m high, 580 m span).
  • Puli Bridge: Fixed typo “Pull Bridge” → “Puli Bridge” on Gallery.

Tallest Bridges Top 10 update: Changtai & Ma’anshan lead, plus #10 Xiang River fix

Changelog · Applied 27 November 2026 · Pages updated: Tallest Bridges, History

Updated Tallest Bridges (by structural tower/pylon height) and matching History records:

  • Changtai Yangtze River Bridge (Jiangsu, China) — Added at #1: Opened to road traffic on 9 September 2025 with 352 m (1,155 ft) pylons and a 1,176 m cable-stayed main span, surpassing Millau Viaduct (343 m).
  • Ma’anshan Yangtze River Rail-Road Bridge (Anhui, China) — Added at #2: Structural closure in 2025 ahead of 2026 operations, with a 345 m (1,132 ft) middle tower (dual 1,120 m main spans).
  • Millau Viaduct (#1 → #3): Updated description on Tallest Bridges and History to reflect its 2004–2025 reign (343 m mast / 336.4 m structural height). (Note: Zhangjinggao Yangtze River Bridge topped out 350 m towers in late 2025 ahead of a ~2028 opening.)
  • Xiang River Bridge (old #10 removed from Top 10 & corrected): Previous card wrongly listed Hunan, China (Opened: 2012) with 132 m / 300 m spans. The 288 m bridge is the Zunyu Expressway Xiang River Bridge in Guizhou, China, opened in 2021 with a 560 m main span (now #12 outside the Top 10 alongside Chishi Bridge at #11).
  • Pingtang Bridge (#5): Corrected deck description on Tallest Bridges from concrete beam to steel-concrete composite girder (with the world’s tallest 332 m concrete tower and dual 550 m spans).
  • Yavuz Sultan Selim Bridge (#7): Updated type metadata Cable-stayed bridge → Hybrid cable-stayed / suspension bridge on Tallest Bridges.
  • Stonecutters Bridge (#10): Corrected opening date on History from April 7, 2009 (deck closure) to December 20, 2009 (traffic opening), and clarified ~293.5 m tower height.

Husutong Yangtze River Bridge opened in 2020, not 2009

Changelog · Applied earlier & verified 27 November 2026 · Pages updated: Tallest Bridges

The Husutong Yangtze River Bridge (Hushutong) in Jiangsu, China is a combined rail-and-road cable-stayed crossing of the Yangtze with towers measuring about 325–330 m (1,066–1,080 ft).

  • What changed: Opened year 2009 → 2020 (opened to traffic in June–July 2020).
  • Ranking update (27 Nov 2026): Moved from #4 to #6 following the additions of Changtai and Ma’anshan Yangtze River Bridges.

Akashi Kaikyō Bridge suspension span record updated

Changelog · Applied 27 November 2026 · Pages updated: Gallery, History

  • What changed: Updated Gallery and History highlights from “currently the world’s longest suspension bridge (1,991 m)” to note that Japan’s Akashi Kaikyō Bridge held the record from 1998 until 2022, when surpassed by Turkey’s 1915 Çanakkale Bridge (2,023 m).
