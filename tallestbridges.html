Top 10 Tallest Bridges in the World

1. Changtai Yangtze River Bridge (China)

Height: 352 m (1,155 ft)
Type: Cable-stayed bridge (road-rail)
Location: Jiangsu, China
Opened: 2025

Opened on 9 September 2025, the Changtai Yangtze River Bridge connects Changzhou and Taizhou with diamond-shaped pylons rising 352 meters—surpassing the Millau Viaduct as the world's tallest bridge structure—alongside the world's longest cable-stayed main span at 1,176 meters. See changelog update.

2. Ma'anshan Yangtze River Rail-Road Bridge (China)

Height: 345 m (1,132 ft)
Type: Three-tower cable-stayed bridge (road-rail)
Location: Anhui, China
Opened: 2025–2026

The Ma'anshan Yangtze River Rail-Road Bridge is a three-tower, dual 1,120-meter main-span cable-stayed crossing carrying both expressway and high-speed rail decks, with its middle tower reaching approximately 345 meters (structural closure achieved in 2025 ahead of full 2026 operations). See changelog update.

3. Millau Viaduct (France)

Height: 343 m (1,125 ft) mast (336.4 m structural)
Type: Cable-stayed bridge
Location: Millau, France
Opened: 2004

The Millau Viaduct held the record as the tallest bridge in the world from 2004 until 2025, spanning the Tarn River Valley in southern France. Its tallest mast reaches 343 meters (1,125 ft) above the valley floor, taller than the Eiffel Tower, before being surpassed by China's Changtai Yangtze River Bridge. Why the ranking changed.

4. 1915 Çanakkale Bridge (Turkey)

Height: 334 m (1,096 ft) (318 m towers + 16 m ornaments)
Type: Suspension bridge
Location: Çanakkale, Turkey
Opened: 2022

The 1915 Çanakkale Bridge connects the European and Asian sides of Turkey across the Dardanelles Strait. It's the longest suspension bridge in the world with a main span of 2,023 meters (6,637 ft).

5. Pingtang Bridge (China)

Height: 332 m (1,089 ft)
Type: Cable-stayed bridge
Location: Guizhou, China
Opened: 2019

The Pingtang Bridge features the tallest concrete bridge tower in the world (332 m) supporting dual 550-meter main spans with a steel-concrete composite girder deck across the Caodu River canyon in Guizhou province. Deck type corrected.

6. Husutong Yangtze River Bridge (China)

Height: 325–330 m (1,066–1,080 ft)
Type: Cable-stayed bridge
Location: Jiangsu, China
Opened: 2020

The Husutong Yangtze River Bridge is a combined rail and road bridge that forms a critical link in China's transportation network. Its height allows large ships to pass underneath on the busy Yangtze River. Opening year corrected to 2020. See the fact update.

7. Yavuz Sultan Selim Bridge (Turkey)

Height: 322 m (1,056 ft)
Type: Hybrid cable-stayed / suspension bridge
Location: Istanbul, Turkey
Opened: 2016

The Yavuz Sultan Selim Bridge is a hybrid cable-stayed suspension bridge that carries both highway and railway traffic across the Bosphorus. It's the third bridge connecting the European and Asian sides of Istanbul. Type metadata updated.

8. Russky Bridge (Russia)

Height: 320.9 m (1,053 ft)
Type: Cable-stayed bridge
Location: Vladivostok, Russia
Opened: 2012

The Russky Bridge connects Russky Island to the mainland near Vladivostok. Built for the 2012 APEC summit, it features one of the longest cable-stayed spans in the world at 1,104 meters (3,622 ft).

9. Sutong Bridge (China)

Height: 306 m (1,004 ft)
Type: Cable-stayed bridge
Location: Jiangsu, China
Opened: 2008

The Sutong Bridge was the world's longest cable-stayed bridge when completed, with a main span of 1,088 meters (3,570 ft). It crosses the Yangtze River and significantly reduced travel time between Nantong and Suzhou.

10. Stonecutters Bridge (Hong Kong)

Height: 298 m (978 ft) (~293.5 m towers)
Type: Cable-stayed bridge
Location: Hong Kong
Opened: 2009

The Stonecutters Bridge is a landmark cable-stayed bridge in Hong Kong, opened to traffic in December 2009. Its innovative design features twin single-leg towers that lean outwards, creating a distinctive silhouette against the Hong Kong skyline. (Chishi Bridge and the 2021 Zunyu Expressway Xiang River Bridge in Guizhou now sit just outside the Top 10; see changelog.)
